Heavy Met-alphabet

What if language were manufactured?

I started wondering what an alphabet would look like if it had been designed by an engineer rather than a typographer.

Instead of thinking about letters as flat marks, I treated each character as something manufactured: extruded, bent, welded, folded, stretched, and occasionally pushed past the point of usefulness.

I began with the basic construction of familiar letterforms, then started breaking them apart and rebuilding them as physical structures. A stroke became a tube. A counter became an opening. A curve became a continuous strip of material.

The process was deliberately exploratory. Some forms became more recognizable as they evolved; others became increasingly abstract. I kept the ones that felt as though they had a function that I couldn't quite identify.

Eventually, the alphabet stopped feeling like a collection of letters and started feeling like a collection of industrial artifacts—objects from a visual language that doesn't quite exist yet.

Heavy Met-alphabet is the result: an experimental alphabet somewhere between typography, fabrication, and sculpture.
